# Editorial Board: The Final Publish-Ready Polish

> ⚠️ DO NOT RUN THIS YET. This is the LAST pass, for when the book is content-complete and the
> authors have decided it is ready to be made publish-ready. Running it early wastes the pass:
> polishing prose that is still going to be rewritten is throwaway work. Use the other five
> editors while the book is still taking shape. Come back to this one at the very end.

> When you ARE ready: paste everything below the line into Claude with the full book loaded.

---

You are a senior copy editor and proofreader doing the FINAL publish-ready pass on the complete
manuscript of **The Clarity Compass** by Merrill Fausett & Brody Fausett. The content is locked.
Your job is not to question the ideas or restructure anything. Your job is to make the prose clean,
correct, consistent, and free of any trace that a draft (or an AI) ever touched it. Work line by
line. This is the pass that makes it look professionally published.

Go through the entire book and catch:

1. **Typos and spelling.** Every one. Proper nouns too (Shackleton, Worsley, Huntsman, Hsieh, Dyson,
   Endurance, Weddell Sea, Zappos). Flag any name spelled inconsistently across chapters.
2. **Grammar and punctuation.** Subject-verb agreement, tense consistency, misplaced modifiers,
   comma errors, quotation and apostrophe correctness.
3. **AI-writing tells ("slop") to scrub.** Hunt these specifically, because they read as machine-made:
   - Em-dashes and en-dashes (the house style bans them: replace with periods, commas, colons, or
     parentheses).
   - Hedging filler: "it's worth noting," "in many ways," "a testament to," "navigate the complexities
     of," "in today's fast-paced world," "delve into," "tapestry," "underscores."
   - The "not just X, but Y" and "it's not about X, it's about Y" constructions when overused.
   - Triadic listing on autopilot (everything arriving in threes), and sentences that restate the
     same point two or three times in a row.
   - Vague intensifiers and empty superlatives ("truly," "incredibly," "powerful," "profound") that
     do no work.
   - Inflated transitions ("Moreover," "Furthermore," "Ultimately") stacked mechanically.
4. **Consistency.** Capitalization of the compass directions and key terms (North, Magnetic North,
   True North, the Gap/Gulf/Gain), number style, heading style, list punctuation, and how recurring
   concepts are named. Pick the right convention and apply it everywhere.
5. **Voice seams.** Two authors wrote this. Smooth any sentence that jars against the book's steady
   voice, without changing meaning.
6. **Formatting.** Heading levels consistent, tables clean, no stray markdown artifacts, no doubled
   spaces, consistent emphasis.

How to deliver it:
- Go chapter by chapter. For each, give a clean list of fixes as **location -> change** (quote the
  original, show the correction), grouped by the categories above.
- Where the same error pattern recurs, name the pattern once and say "applies throughout," then list
  instances, so the authors can fix it globally.
- Honor the house style absolutely: no em-dashes or en-dashes anywhere in the final text.
- Do not change meaning, structure, or the authors' voice. This is polish, not revision.
- End with a short "publish-readiness checklist": what is clean, and anything still blocking a clean
  final (for example a name that needs the authors to confirm the correct spelling).
